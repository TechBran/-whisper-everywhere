# The 8 Gen 2 runs the NPU turbo tier — the experiment `CPU_BY_CENSUS` asked for

**Result: PASS.** A QAIRT context binary compiled for `soc_model 43` / `htp_version 73` loads and
executes on a retail SM8550, and the whole NPU pipeline — sentence timestamps, speaker labels,
live words — works on it.

**Device:** Galaxy S23 Ultra, `SM-S918U`, `SOC_MODEL=SM8550`, platform `kalama`, Android 16,
7,242,344 kB RAM (against the Fold6's 12 GB).
**Build:** 4.11.3 debug off branch `spike/8gen2-qcs8550`, sideloaded after uninstalling the Play
copy (owner-authorised).
**Pack:** AI Hub **v0.62.2** `qcs8550-proxy`, `npu-turbo`, w8a16, `adb push`ed into
`files/models/` under the delivery names and sha256-verified on the device.
**Session:** 2026-09-22 11:50:42 → 11:52:20, 12 committed chunks.

## 1. What the census asked, and what answers it

`NpuFleetCensus.CPU_BY_CENSUS` has carried this since 2026-08-29:

> **The cross-load curiosity is OUT.** The 7 Gen 4's binaries are compiled for HTP v73 — the same
> architecture as the 8 Gen 2 — but for the vendor's `soc_model` 86, and whether they load on an
> SM8550 is unverified (`QNN_COMMON_ERROR_INCOMPATIBLE_BINARIES` risk). Its trigger, so the next
> reader knows what would reopen it: an 8 Gen 2 device materialising for the 30-minute experiment.

Two things changed. The device materialised. And **the cross-load is no longer the question**: AI
Hub v0.62.2 publishes a `qcs8550-proxy` package whose `metadata.json` reads `soc_model 43` /
`htp_version 73` — the SM8550's own number, where the rejected cross-load was built for 86. The
census's specific objection does not apply to this artifact.

| | our 7 Gen 4 pack | the qcs8550 pack |
|---|---|---|
| `htp_version` | 73 | 73 |
| `soc_model` | **86** | **43** |
| reference device | Snapdragon 7 Gen 4 QRD | QCS8550 (Proxy) |

## 2. It was offered, and it ran

```
npu: offer soc=SM8550:pass probe=pass installed=npu,npu-turbo offered=npu,npu-turbo
encode: graphExecute OK in 2612.1 ms (vote: OK sustained: dcvsEnable=1 PERFORMANCE …)
```

The SoC gate passed, the native probe passed, and both tiers were offered. No
`INCOMPATIBLE_BINARIES`, no `stage=skel` failure, no fallback to CPU.

## 3. Throughput — it keeps up with margin

12 chunks, all terminated by EOT at temperature rung 0 (the repetition ladder never engaged):

| | S23 Ultra (8 Gen 2, v73) | Fold6 (8 Elite, v79) |
|---|---|---|
| encode | p50 **2,472 ms**, p90 2,547, max 2,610 | p50 1,874 ms |
| decode | p50 **452 ms**, max 1,143 (at 94 tokens) | p50 209 ms |
| total per chunk | p50 **2,924 ms**, p90 3,223, max 3,617 | ~2,083 ms |
| share of the 8 s commit floor | **37%** p50, 45% worst | 26% |

**1.40× the Fold6's per-chunk cost, with 55-63% of the budget still unspent.** Older silicon, one
HTP generation back, and it is not close to falling behind. The owner's own report: *"it felt
really fast."*

## 4. The whole pipeline works, not just the encode

- **Sentence-granularity speaker windows**: `windows=` p50 **3**, max **8**, on `route=vad`. At
  4.10.1 this tier returned 1 window per chunk, always.
- **A speaker change inside one chunk** — the 4.11.0 capability, on this device:
  `speaker: seq=9 segs=2 windows=4 embedMs=308 ids=[2,1,1,1] best=[0.48,0.62,0.59,0.65]`
- **Two speakers detected and held**; the retrospective pass ran twice and answered 2 clusters
  both times, changing nothing.
- **Live words**: `stream-gate … -> preview=1`, then
  `stream-open: sherpa=1.13.7 ort=1.27.1 threads=2 canary=pass canaryMs=484`.
- Fingerprinting cost p50 ~300 ms, well inside the finalize fence.

The four `dlopen … absolute path failed` lines are the loader's documented by-soname fallback
succeeding afterwards, not failures — everything downstream ran.

## 5. What this is worth

The SM8550 is the **S23, S23+ and S23 Ultra**, and every other 8 Gen 2 phone. That is a large
slice of the fleet currently answering CPU, and the NPU tier is the one the owner reports nobody
complains about. It reaches those users **locally and free** — no GPU tier, no subscription, no
audio leaving the device.

## 6. What the spike deliberately does NOT have

Branch `spike/8gen2-qcs8550` exists to answer one question on one phone. Before any of this can
ship it needs, and none of it is done:

- a **device-group XML** entry for `soc_qcs8550`, and a pack variant directory
- a `build_asset_packs.py` `FAMILIES` key, so the pack is fetched and measured by the tool rather
  than by hand
- **every pinned test updated** — `NpuPackLayoutTest`, the census/ledger disjointness tests, the
  htp/packGroup pairing pins
- **the 0.62.2 re-measurement.** This pack is v0.62.2; the other four families are measured
  against v0.61.0, and `build_asset_packs.py` asserts one release string for the whole census. A
  fifth family from a different release cannot be added without resolving that — the guard that
  refused to build is the same guard that will refuse this.
- a second device, or at least a second session, before a fleet-wide claim rests on one phone
- the 80-bin **small** tier on this family is untested: it failed at `stage=mel` for want of a
  donor model on a wiped device, which is a separate and already-understood thing

## 7. One incidental finding worth keeping

The NPU **small** tier cannot start on a device with no ggml model installed: it is 80-bin and
borrows its filterbank from any installed 80-bin tier's file, and a fresh install has none.
`npu-turbo` is 128-bin and 4.1 bundles `melbank-128.bin` in the APK for exactly this reason, so it
has no such dependency. Normal onboarding installs a CPU model first, so this is only reachable on
a wiped device — but it is why the first attempt here failed, and it means **turbo is the
better tier to test on a clean device**, not merely the more interesting one.
