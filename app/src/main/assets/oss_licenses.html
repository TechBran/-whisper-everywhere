What these repositories supply as a notice, and what they do not. The Apache License 2.0 asks a distributor to retain the copyright, patent, trademark and attribution notices a work carries (§4(c)), and to reproduce the contents of a NOTICE text file where the work includes one (§4(d)). The MIT License asks for its copyright line. None of the seven repositories above contains a NOTICE, LICENSE or COPYING file. That is read from each repository's complete recursive file listing at the revision pinned on its row. Six of the seven declare their grant as a bare identifier in the model card's own front matter — license: apache-2.0, or license: mit for Indonesian — which carries no copyright line, no holder and no year. The seventh is the Russian row: the mirror its bytes come from declares nothing of its own at all, and the grant relied on is the one on the tagged upstream named on that row, to which its four files are byte-identical. So §4(d) has no NOTICE text to carry and §4(c) no copyright notice to retain beyond the declaration itself — and where none is supplied, none has been invented here. What each repository does supply is reproduced on its row: the declaration, the revision, and the corpora its publisher names. Where one supplies a little more it is noted as theirs rather than ours — the Korean repository publishes a SHA256SUMS file beside its weights, and its model card's own License section reads, in full, “Apache 2.0.”

What we changed, and what we did not

The Apache License 2.0 asks that modified files carry prominent notice of the modification (§4(b)), so here is everything that happens to these weights between the repository and your device — including the parts where the answer is nothing.

The weights themselves are not modified. Each of the 28 files — four per language — is downloaded byte-for-byte from the pinned revision named on its row above, and is refused unless its sha256 matches a digest recorded in this app's own source code. That check runs when the pack is assembled, again from disk before a release is built, and again on your device before a byte is installed. We do not re-train, re-export, re-quantise or otherwise alter them. Where a filename says int8, that quantisation is the upstream publisher's own export and not ours — and one of these files is not quantised at all, the Russian pack's decoder.onnx.

What we do change is selection, naming and packaging.

  • Selection — four files out of each repository, not the repository. Several hold a great deal more: the German one also publishes a 1.6 GB decoding graph and a 1.0 GB training checkpoint, which this app never downloads.
  • Naming — two of the seven keep their files in subdirectories, and we place them under flat names. The German pack's four come from exp/epoch-30/ and lang_bpe_500/, and their ONNX filenames carry commas that an Android asset entry cannot; the Chinese-English pack's come from exp/32/ and data/lang_char_bpe/. Only where the bytes sit and what they are called changes — never the bytes.
  • Packaging — the four files become one on-demand Android asset pack per language, installed into this app's own private storage.
